# Findings Granada W

## Clustering results

For k = 2, Granada were clustered with front footed teams. Then at k = 3 and k = 4, they were clustered with passing teams. The margin was clear but modest for every k.

### Like front footed teams

Granada stood out for more progressive passes and progression via passing. Their goal kicks were mostly long. They were less patient in building up towards shots. They had more box entries via passing, especially with crosses. They were more aggressive with counterpress actions on opposition half.

### Unlike front footed teams

Granada shot more from open play, and less from set piece and counter attacks. They were less direct in their attacks.

### Like passing teams

Granada moved up and entered the box more through passing. Their shots came more from open play and less from set piece.

### Unlike passing teams

Granada crossed more, played longer goal kicks, and counterpressed more than most passing teams did.

## Pitch profile

Most of their final third entries and touches were on wide areas, with the right flank being preferred. Only a fourth of their attacks went through the middle.

## Formations and typical XI

Granada mainly split between 4-4-2 and 4-2-3-1 formations, while in 13% of the time they used back 3 formations and in 12% of the time they used wingerless formations, with 3-5-2 being the third most used formation.

For their main formation 4-4-2, the positions that saw significant rotations were one of the center backs, right back, and left winger.

## Similar teams

Sevilla were the closest team to Granada in terms of squad, while Freiburg were the closest on style. Overall, Frankfurt were the closest team to Granada, which were the only team that were close to Granada on both squad and style.
