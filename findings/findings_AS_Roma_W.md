# Findings AS Roma W

## Clustering results

At k = 2, Roma were safely clustered with front-footed teams. Then at k = 3 and k = 4, Roma were safely clustered with direct teams.

### Like its cluster

For a team of their strength, Roma were more direct and less patient in building up, while they did not shoot much from open play but more from direct set piece and transitions. Roma also held a high defensive line and were active in counterpress actions in opposition half. They also carried much into the box.

### Unlike its cluster

Roma were not as progressive with their passes as their cluster does, as more of their progression and box entries were done via carrying. Roma did not attempt many long goal kicks and pass into the final third through the middle.

## Pitch profile

Most of their final third entries and touches were on wide areas, particularly on the right. Both final third entries and touches on the left were more than centrally, although the gap was much smaller when it comes to touches.

## Formations and typical XI

Roma mostly uses the 4-3-3 formation. They used back three formations 20% of the time and wingerless formations 19% of the time, with 3-5-2 being their second most used formation.

In their 4-3-3 formation, their goalkeeper of Ceasar, their center back pairing of Minami and Linari, their single pivot of Kumagai, and their striker of Giacinti were the more consistent positions. Di Guglielmo was a regular in the side, although she rotated between left back and right back. Giugliano was commonly used as one of the number 8 midfielders, usually more on the right. Haavi was mostly played as a left winger, although she was also a common option for right winger.

## Similar teams

Brighton are the only team close to Roma in terms of both squad and style, in which squad is the main contributor. Roma are not similar to any teams when it comes to style.
